# Theoretical Lineages by Domain

For the `/review-landscape` skill. Load when dispatching Wave 2 (theoretical pillars) subagents. These founder chains are the search seeds — use instead of generic field defaults.

## Output form: Multi-trunk ancestry tree (preferred)

When the field is mature (2+ parent disciplines, ancestors pre-1950), Phase 4b should produce a **Haberl-2019-style genealogical tree** rather than a flat founder list. Reference: Haberl et al. 2019 *Nature Sustainability* "Contributions of sociometabolic research to sustainability science" — Figure 2 traces sociometabolic research through two trunks (social sciences / natural sciences) back to Adam Smith 1776. Each ancestor node: `**Author YEAR** — [contribution in <15 words]`.

For young fields (<30 years of ancestry), fall back to flat founder chain + named laws.

### Founder chain

## Urban science

### Founder chain
George Kingsley Zipf → Walter Christaller → Paul Krugman → **Luis Bettencourt** & **Jose Lobo** → **Edward Glaeser** → Michael Batty / **Jane Jacobs** (cross-generational influence)

### Named laws & frameworks
- **Zipf's law for cities** — rank-size distribution
- **Bettencourt 2007 urban scaling** — β ≈ 1.12 (wages); β ≈ 1.13–1.27 (GDP/patents); β ≈ 0.85 (infrastructure)
- **Christaller central-place theory**
- **Urban agglomeration economies** — Marshall 1890 / Jacobs 1969 / Glaeser 2011

### Search-seed papers

- Bettencourt 2013 "The origins of scaling in cities" — *Science*

## Urban sustainability & industrial ecology — DUAL-TRUNK TREE

These two domains share a dual parent lineage per Haberl et al. 2019. Tree structure (two trunks meeting at modern sociometabolic research / industrial ecology / urban metabolism):

### Social sciences trunk (left — cultural anthropology, political economy, sociology)

- **A. Smith (1776)** — Source of value: human labour
- **H. Spencer (1862)** — Available energy surplus determines societal advancement
- **K. Marx & F. Engels (1867)** — Labour achieves metabolism between man and nature; exchange vs. use value
- **L. White (1949)** — Energy per capita drives cultural evolution
- **F. Cottrell (1955)** — Energy (EROI) limits range of societal activities
- **M. Harris & R. Rapaport (1966)** — Metabolic needs of population translate into cultural practices
- **K. Boulding (1966)** — Cowboy economy on spaceship Earth must fail
- **W. Leontief (1966)** — Input–output analysis of national economics
- **N. Georgescu-Roegen (1969)** — Economics ignores the entropy law
- **D. & D. Meadows (1972)** — Limits to (biophysical) growth

### Natural sciences trunk (right — physical geography, physics, chemistry, geology, ecology)

- **J. Moleschott (1852)** — Materialism: no phosphorus, no thought; metabolism
- **G.P. Marsh (1864)** — Physical geography modified by human action (forest decline)
- **P. Geddes (1884)** — Economy of energy and resources: physical input–output table (proto-Leontief)
- **N. Shaler (1905)** — Quantifies human consumption of minerals
- **W. Ostwald (1909)** — Energetic imperative for society: efficient use, solar energy at last
- **F. Soddy (1912)** — Thermodynamics applies to society: no long-term economic growth
- **A. Lotka (1925)** — Law of maximum energy in biological systems
- **R. Lindemann (1942)** — Analyzes ecosystems as energy conversion
- **A. Wolman (1965)** — (Water) metabolism of cities
- **R.U. Ayres (1968)** — Economics at variance with law of conservation of mass
- **H.R. Maturana & F.J. Varela (1972)** — Autopoietic systems maintaining and reproducing themselves
- **E.P. & H.T. Odum (1973)** — Energy: embodied energy, energy used in the past

### Modern synthesis (2000s–present)

These ancestors converge in current Social Metabolism Research / Industrial Ecology / Urban Metabolism via:
- **R. Frosch & N. Gallopoulos (1989)** — Industrial symbiosis as manufacturing strategy
- **R.U. Ayres & A. Kneese (1969)** — Production, consumption, and externalities (mass balance)
- **T. Graedel & B. Allenby (1995)** — *Industrial Ecology* textbook consolidation
- **M. Fischer-Kowalski & H. Haberl (2007)** — Socioecological transitions (MuSIASEM)
- **C. Kennedy (2007)** — Changing metabolism of cities
- **N. Grimm et al. (2008)** — Global change and the ecology of cities
- **Ellen MacArthur Foundation (2013)** — Circular economy framing
- **H. Haberl et al. (2019)** *Nat Sustain* — canonical lineage mapping itself

### Named laws & frameworks

- **Wolman 1965 urban metabolism** — cities as metabolic organisms
- **Frosch-Gallopoulos 1989 industrial symbiosis** — waste of one = input of another
- **Ayres-Kneese mass balance** — all inputs must equal outputs + accumulation
- **Kaya identity** — emissions = population × GDP/person × energy/GDP × emissions/energy
- **Lotka maximum-power principle** — ecosystems maximize energy throughput
- **Georgescu-Roegen entropy constraint** — economic activity irreversibly increases entropy
- **Limits to Growth** — Meadows exponential vs. carrying capacity

### Search-seed papers
- Haberl, Wiedenhofer, Pauliuk, Krausmann, Müller, Fischer-Kowalski 2019 "Contributions of sociometabolic research to sustainability science" — *Nature Sustainability* [CANONICAL LINEAGE REFERENCE]
- Wolman 1965 "The Metabolism of Cities" — *Scientific American*
- Kennedy, Cuddihy, Engel-Yan 2007 "The changing metabolism of cities" — *Journal of Industrial Ecology*
- Grimm et al. 2008 "Global Change and the Ecology of Cities" — *Science*
- Frosch & Gallopoulos 1989 "Strategies for Manufacturing" — *Scientific American*
- Ayres & Kneese 1969 "Production, Consumption, and Externalities" — *AER*
- Georgescu-Roegen 1971 *The Entropy Law and the Economic Process*
- Meadows et al. 1972 *The Limits to Growth*

## Economic geography

### Founder chain
Alfred Marshall 1890 → **William Skinner 1964** (hierarchical marketing systems, Chinese context) / Walter Christaller 1933 → **Paul Krugman 1991** (new economic geography) → **Peter Taylor** & **Ben Derudder** (GaWC, world cities network) → **Luis Bettencourt** & **Jose Lobo** (scaling / econ bridge) → **Edward Glaeser** (agglomeration) / **Jane Jacobs** (urban economic diversity)

### Named laws & frameworks
- **Krugman 1991 core-periphery** — increasing returns + transport costs → agglomeration
- **Skinner 1964 central-place hierarchies** — macroregional system of Chinese market towns
- **Taylor-Derudder world city network** — inter-city service flows, GaWC roster
- **Jane Jacobs externalities** — diversity drives growth (vs. Marshall-Arrow-Romer specialization)
- **Glaeser agglomeration wage premium** — human capital externalities
- **Zipf-Gibrat proportionate growth** — rank-size of city distribution

### Search-seed papers
- Krugman 1991 "Increasing Returns and Economic Geography" — *Journal of Political Economy*
- Skinner 1964 "Marketing and Social Structure in Rural China" — *J. Asian Studies* (3-part series)
- Taylor 2004 *World City Network: A Global Urban Analysis*
- Derudder & Taylor 2020 "Three globalizations shaping the twenty-first century" — *Urban Geography*
- Jacobs 1969 *The Economy of Cities*
- Glaeser, Kallal, Scheinkman, Shleifer 1992 "Growth in Cities" — *JPE*
