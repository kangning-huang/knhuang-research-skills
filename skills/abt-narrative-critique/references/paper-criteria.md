# Paper Evaluation Criteria

Papers report completed research. The ABT structure must convince reviewers and readers that the work advances knowledge and merits publication.

## AND: Introduction Framing

**What to check:**
- Does the opening establish why readers should care?
- Is the literature review focused on the specific research question, not the general field?
- Are citations accurate? (This is where distortions most commonly occur)
- Does the framing position the work within ongoing scientific conversations?

**Citation density norms (introduction only):**
- Nature/Science: 5-8 highly focused citations
- Nature-family journals (Nat. Clim. Change, Nat. Cities): 10-15 citations
- Specialty journals (Urban Climate, Building & Environment): 15-25 citations
- Review-style introductions in research articles: problematic if >30

**Journal-specific considerations:**
- High-impact journals want broad significance framing
- Specialty journals want technical depth and methodological positioning
- Interdisciplinary journals need accessible framing for non-specialists

**Fatal flaws:**
- Citations misrepresent source findings
- Key recent papers ignored (especially if they challenge the premise)
- Introduction frames a different question than the paper answers

**Significant weaknesses:**
- Over-citation (every sentence has 3+ citations)
- Literature review is comprehensive but unfocused
- Missing connection to broader significance

## BUT: Gap Identification for Publishability

**What to check:**
- Is there a clear pivot from "what we know" to "what we don't know"?
- Is the gap specific and falsifiable?
- Does the gap motivate the specific study design used?
- Could the gap have been stated before the literature review? (If yes, the AND section isn't earning its place)

**Gap statement quality markers:**
Strong: "While X has been established, the role of Y remains unclear because Z"
Weak: "Few studies have examined X" (quantity ≠ gap)
Fatal: No explicit gap statement

**Significance articulation:**
The gap should explain consequences of the knowledge gap:
- For theory: "This limits our ability to predict/explain..."
- For methods: "This prevents us from accurately measuring..."
- For applications: "This hampers efforts to..."

**Fatal flaws:**
- No gap statement (introduction ends with "In this study, we...")
- Gap is post-hoc rationalization that doesn't match the actual study
- Gap is trivially filled by the existence of any study

**Significant weaknesses:**
- Gap significance asserted but not argued
- Gap too narrow for the journal's scope
- Gap could be addressed with existing methods (so why this study?)

## THEREFORE: Results and Implications

**What to check:**
- Do results actually address the stated gap?
- Is the "glove-to-hand" fit demonstrated, not just claimed?
- Are implications proportionate to findings?
- Does the discussion return to the gap and explain how it's now filled?

**Results-gap alignment:**
Map each major finding back to the gap:
- Finding X addresses aspect Y of the gap
- If findings don't map cleanly, the ABT structure needs revision

**Implication calibration:**
- Are claims supported by the evidence, or over-reaching?
- Do broader implications acknowledge limitations?
- Is the "so what" clear without being grandiose?

**For high-impact journals:**
The THEREFORE must show paradigm-level implications:
- Changes how we think about a problem
- Opens new research directions
- Has policy/practice implications

**Fatal flaws:**
- Results don't address the stated gap
- Claims exceed evidence
- No synthesis—just results followed by boilerplate implications

**Significant weaknesses:**
- Implications are vague ("contributes to understanding")
- Missing limitations discussion
- New questions raised but not connected to findings
