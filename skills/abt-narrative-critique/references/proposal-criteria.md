# Proposal Evaluation Criteria

Proposals are forward-looking documents. The ABT structure must convince reviewers that the research is worth funding.

## AND: Literature Review Quality

**What to check:**
- Does the framing establish the field's current state without being exhaustive?
- Are seminal works cited alongside recent advances?
- Is the scope appropriate for the funding call? (NSFC vs. NSF vs. foundation grants have different expectations)
- Does the literature build toward the gap, or feel like a disconnected list?

**Citation density norms:**
- NSFC proposals: 15-25 references typical; introduction section 5-10 key citations
- NSF proposals: 20-40 references; broader impact framing expected
- Foundation grants: Often shorter; 10-15 focused citations

**Fatal flaws:**
- Missing foundational works that any reviewer would expect
- Over-citing the PI's own work without establishing broader context
- Literature review that doesn't connect to the proposed research

**Significant weaknesses:**
- Dated citations in rapidly evolving fields (nothing post-2020 in AI/ML, urban heat)
- Imbalanced coverage (strong on methods literature, weak on application domain)

## BUT: Gap Statement for Fundability

**What to check:**
- Is the gap specific enough to be addressable in the proposed timeline?
- Is it broad enough to justify the requested budget?
- Does the gap statement avoid trivially true claims ("no one has studied X in city Y")?
- Is the significance articulated in terms reviewers care about?

**Significance framing:**
For environmental/urban science proposals, gaps should connect to:
1. Scientific advancement (new theory, mechanisms, understanding)
2. Methodological innovation (new techniques, data fusion, validation approaches)
3. Societal impact (climate adaptation, public health, environmental justice)
4. Policy relevance (actionable for urban planners, policymakers)

**Fatal flaws:**
- No clear gap statement (jumps from background to methods)
- Gap is trivial ("no one has done exactly this before")
- Gap disconnected from literature review

**Significant weaknesses:**
- Gap significance stated but not argued
- Multiple gaps dilute focus
- Gap too ambitious for scope/timeline

## THEREFORE: Proposed Research Fit

**What to check:**
- Do proposed methods directly address the stated gap?
- Is there a clear logic chain: gap → approach → expected outcomes?
- Are preliminary results sufficient to demonstrate feasibility?
- Does the timeline match the scope?

**Method-gap alignment tests:**
- Can you complete this sentence: "Because [GAP], we will [METHOD], which will reveal [OUTCOME]"?
- Would the proposed methods answer a different question equally well? (If yes, alignment is weak)
- Are the methods novel enough to justify funding, or are they routine applications?

**Expected outcomes:**
Proposals should articulate:
- Specific deliverables (datasets, models, publications)
- How results will advance the field
- Broader impacts (training, outreach, policy engagement)

**Fatal flaws:**
- Methods don't address the stated gap
- No preliminary data for high-risk approaches
- Outcomes are vague ("will contribute to understanding")

**Significant weaknesses:**
- Methods are incremental rather than innovative
- Timeline unrealistic
- Broader impacts feel like afterthoughts
